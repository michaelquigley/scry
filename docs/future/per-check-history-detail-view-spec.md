# Per-Check History Detail View

The strip answers the glance question: how often, how long, and was anyone watching. It does not answer the follow-up — what actually happened, in order, in the daemon's own words. That answer already exists: the ledger records every transition with the kind it fired under and the detail that caused it, and `GET /api/history` serves it across any window with per-event `kind` and `detail`. What is missing is a surface for it.

The eye pass found the strip's first flaw at width in the same sitting: a full-width band reads as a separator line between checks, not as data. Two fixes land in one surface. The strip's left edge moves to the check column's left edge, and the detail panel inherits that alignment, so the page's two history surfaces read as one.

## The Surface

The page stays one page with no router. Clicking a check's strip opens a detail panel that expands beneath that check's row, nested under the check column. One panel is open at a time; clicking the open check's strip again closes it, and clicking another check's strip moves it. The table remains the page's single spine, and its trouble-first order is untouched. A dialog was considered and rejected: it would reintroduce the full-width chrome the eye pass just removed, and it would sever the panel from the check it describes.

The panel is per-check but its document is per-estate: a history document carries every configured check across the window, so opening a different check's panel reuses the held document, and only a window change fetches. When the panel's window is the row strip's own 90 days, the panel renders from the strip's held document — the same cut as the row beneath it, so the two can never disagree — including the pairing of that document's own arrival instant, which is what the list's ages count from and which status polls thereafter leave untouched.

The panel, top to bottom: a wider, taller strip over the selected window; a row of window presets; the transition list.

## The Window

The presets are spans: `1d`, `14d`, `30d`, `90d`, `180d`, `1y`, and `all`, with `90d` the default, so a panel opened on a check matches the row strip it grew out of. All presets are now-anchored: the right edge is the coverage end of the document the panel renders from — the status document's `generated` for `90d`, which reuses the strip's held document and window, the echoed `to` otherwise — plus its locally elapsed age, the strip's own right-edge law applied to the panel's document, and the left edge is the right edge minus the span. `all` is the one preset with a fixed left bound (2000-01-01T00:00:00Z): the daemon serves whatever the ledger holds, and the years before the ledger's first `start` render unwatched, which is the claim they make — no daemon testifies there. The label is `1d`, matching the page's duration vocabulary, which writes day-scale spans in days; `24h` would be the set's only hour-unit label.

The page sends its bounds explicitly — `from` and `to`, both already declared on the route — and the daemon echoes the window it served. With `to` set to the status document's `generated`, the two documents describe the same window to the millisecond, and the left-edge marker tick that stood in for the few-millisecond prefix between the documents' windows stops existing: the display's left edge is the document's `to` minus the span, exactly the document's `from` at arrival, and as the window slides forward the left edge advances with it over territory the document already covers.

The document's `generated` is the response's stamp, and it is later than the window's end: the daemon reads the clock again after it has resolved the window. The testimony therefore ends at the echoed `to`, and `to` is the watermark the page reads everywhere it vouches, orders in-flight responses, or revalidates a held document; `generated` is a stamp for other consumers, and nothing in the page's logic reads it. With the parameterless fetch the two coincide, which is why the identity was easy to lean on — and why the explicit bounds must retire it.

The wire carries those stamps at whole-second precision — the contract's date-time format truncates the daemon's sub-second instants — so an instant that reads equal to the bound on the wire may be newer in truth. Every comparison the page makes against the bound is at-or-after, never strictly-newer: equality is the case that must dirty, and the cost of the false positive is one poll interval of the honest cap, cleared by the next poll's later bound.

Now-anchoring is law for this surface, not an implementation convenience. Every strip the page renders speaks only for the span its own document covers, and a now-anchored strip adds the tail vouched by the status document, which covers the present that its right edge sits on — and when the polls fail, that same status testimony is what caps both surfaces: while stale, the row strip and the panel cap at the latest good status document's `generated`, the status document still vouching the tail through the outage, and a dirty cap at the echoed `to` takes the earlier of the two. One cap law, two surfaces. A window centered on a past incident is renderable truthfully only when clipped to its own explicitly-bounded document: the bands within `[from, to]` speak for exactly that span, and the tail beyond a historical `to` does not vouch under the current status document — a restart in the seconds after the bound, with no check transition, leaves the current state compatible with the historical tail while the gap is unknowable. So what the preset set defers is scope and interaction, not truth: pan, brush, centering on a selected event, and the extension of a historical window's right edge all defer together, and the question is whether a past window is ever wanted on a strip. The eye pass will judge; until it does, the panel's strip is a zoom on the recent horizon, while the past lives in the list.

## The Transition List

Newest first. The strip already tells the chronological story left to right; the list answers the other question — what happened around the trouble — which lives at the right edge.

The list is scoped to the panel's display window — the same span the strip renders — and slides with it: a row that ages out of the strip's left edge leaves the list too, computed at render time from the held document rather than by a refetch. The window is closed at both ends: a row exactly at either bound belongs to it — with the wire's whole-second stamps, boundary equality is a common case, not an edge. That is what keeps strip and list in agreement while a quiet estate refetches never: one span, two notations. When the display window has slid entirely past what the held document covers, the strip renders fully unwatched and the list shows its empty message; the pair is itself the message, that no daemon testifies for that span, and no machinery is added to say more.

Each transition row carries the timestamp with its age — the daemon's own span to the document's `to`, plus the locally elapsed time since the document arrived, each term in its own clock frame, the page's one age law applied to the list's own document; the state change as a pair of chips; the kind the check had when it fired; the duration the check held the state it left, `ts` minus `prev_since`, straight off the daemon's arithmetic; and the detail string in machine type, wrapping, a dash when absent. Timestamps render in the viewer's local zone as on the rest of the page, and gain the year when the event falls outside the viewer's current calendar year — the `1y` and `all` presets put such events in the list, and `Jan 15, 03:10` is ambiguous across years — while same-year events read exactly as the row strip's do, the age beside each row keeping either form truthful. That arrival is the history document's own: held with the document and never reset by a status poll, so at `90d` it is the strip's held document's arrival, and a page that has held the same document through many quiet polls shows ages that keep counting forward, not resetting.

The daemon's `start` and `stop` rows interleave in time with the check's, rendered muted. They are estate-scoped, not check-scoped, and they earn their place: a gap between two of a check's events is often nobody watching, and the rows say so in the only notation the ledger uses.

Rows that share a wire timestamp — the contract's whole-second stamps make the ledger's sub-second order unrecoverable — break ties in a fixed display order: daemon rows before check rows, check rows by id. That order is a presentation tiebreak, not a claim about sub-second chronology, and it keeps the list deterministic between renders.

A check with no transitions in the window says `no transitions in this window` beneath a strip that still renders its state bands from the bound states. The message is pinned to the check's own rows: it shows whenever and only when the check has no transition rows in the window, daemon rows or no daemon rows — a window holding only estate rows renders those rows and the message beneath them, since the message claims nothing about the daemon, only that the check did not transition. Absence of events is not an error, and the panel says which one it means.

## The Fetch Discipline

No new timer. The detail document rides the existing ten-second poll under its own level-triggered dirty flag, committed before the request and cleared only by a fetch that lands, and revalidated against the newest status on arrival — the row strip's discipline, word for word. A preset change dirties it, as does a `started` that is not the boot the document was fetched under — or that is at or after the document's `to`, the arm that catches a restart the wire truncated into the same second as the bound — as does a `last_transition`, or a new check's `since`, at or after the document's `to`, and a failed fetch dirties it; the at-or-after comparisons are the wire-precision law from The Window, never strict. Two history documents can be in flight, the strip's and the panel's, and each is ordered by its own `to` — the end of the coverage it carries, an equal `to` accepted. The watermark orders responses to the same window, but it cannot say which window a response answered, and a preset change mid-flight leaves a request for an abandoned window in the air: so a panel response is checked twice before it lands. Its echoed bounds must carry the shape of the preset currently selected — the span between them equal to the preset's span, or `all`'s fixed `from` — and its echoed `to` must be at or after the newest status document's `generated`, so it answers the window the page currently asks for rather than merely one of the same shape: a response to an abandoned visit to the same preset (an A→B→A switch with a newer poll in between) carries an older `to` and is rejected, and the dirty flag a failed fetch for the current window left behind stands, so the next poll retries. A mismatch of either kind is ignored outright, leaving the held document and the dirty flag untouched; same-preset responses order by `to` as before, and no request bookkeeping is added — two document comparisons replace what a request counter would track. The strip's responses need no such check: its display window is anchored to the status document's own stamp, so a landing older than the newest status only shortens the history-vouched coverage, the span beyond it being vouched by the status testimony — the strip's window never lags the page's now. Both landing conditions belong to the panel alone.

The panel's flag is revalidated against the newest status on every successful poll whether or not the panel is open: a closed panel fetches nothing — it has nothing to render — but it may not accumulate silence, so its level is maintained through closure, and opening a panel whose flag went dirty during closure kicks the fetch at once, exactly as a preset change does.

After each successful status poll, both dirty levels — the strip's and the panel's — are evaluated and committed synchronously, and both eligible history requests are dispatched before either is awaited. Neither surface may extend obsolete state during the other's in-flight request, and a panel request dispatched from an obsolete status stamp would be rejected by the landing rule and retried with yet another bound — flapping behind the strip for as long as polls pile on — so the evaluation is one pre-request block, not a chain of sequential awaits behind one another's requests.

With the panel closed and no non-default preset ever chosen, the page fetches exactly one history document per refetch — the strip's — and a quiet estate refetches never, as it does today.

## The Row Strip, Revised

The strip row gains an empty cell under `state`, and the strip spans the remaining four columns: its left edge is now the check column's left edge, and its right edge stays at the table's. It reads as data attached to the check rather than a rule between checks. The geometry is untouched — placement is container-relative, so every endpoint stays exact in the narrower container and the marker threshold scales with it — and with the explicit window bounds the left-edge tick stops existing.

## Scenarios

**The flap, examined.** The row strip shows a red band three weeks in. Click it: the panel opens at 90d — the same cut, wider — and the list beneath names it: the failure with the daemon's words, the recovery, the two quiet transitions around it, each with its duration. Switch to `1d`: the panel's strip covers the last day and the list drops to that day's rows.

**The restart inside the story.** A check fails at 02:10, the host dies at 03:00 and boots at 07:30, and the check recovers at 07:31. The panel's list, newest first: recovery, daemon started, daemon stopped, failure. The unwatched band in the panel's strip and the two daemon rows between the check's events tell the same story in two notations.

**The long horizon.** A year in, `all` is chosen. The panel's strip renders the whole ledger, its early years honest hatch — no daemon testifies before the first `start` — and the operator reads the year in the list: every transition, daemon's words, newest first.

**The quiet estate.** Nothing transitioned in the last day. The panel at `1d` says `no transitions in this window` beneath an all-green strip, and the page still refetches never.

## Seam Census

- **model / render** — *unchanged, reinforced.* The explicit `from`/`to` are contract parameters the spec already declares; the daemon still resolves them, echoes them, and owns every state computation; the page learns everything it draws from the document. The choice of window — which span, now-anchored — is a presentation decision, like sorting. Revisit: never.
- **dashboard discipline** — *same laws, new clauses.* The panel obeys the strip's laws: document-only knowledge, one clock frame, unwatched wins, exact geometry, one level-triggered dirty flag, one cadence. This work adds three clauses to the law: the 90d reuse rule, under which the panel renders the strip's held document so panel and row cannot disagree; the now-anchoring of every preset; and per-document vouching, under which each rendered strip speaks only for the span its own document covers — ending at the echoed `to`, the watermark the page reads wherever it vouches, orders, or revalidates — plus the tail beyond the right edge that the row strip has always vouched through the status document's current state and `since` — and the same latest-good-status `generated` caps both the row strip and the panel while polling is failing. The three belong in terminus canon's `dashboard-law` quality at close-out.
- **no contract change.** `from` and `to` are already declared on the route; the hand-written client gains two optional arguments. No `scry.yml` edit, no regeneration, no daemon change.

## Deferred (and Why)

**Incident-centered windows.** Pan, brush, and centering on a selected event are deferred as scope and interaction, not truth: a window around a past span renders truthfully clipped to its own explicitly-bounded document — the bands within `[from, to]` speak for exactly that span — but the tail beyond a historical `to` does not vouch under the current status document, so the extension of a historical window's right edge defers with the interaction itself. The list answers "what happened in that span" in the daemon's words, which covers the retrospective use; the eye pass will show whether a past window is ever wanted on a strip at all.

**`prev_since` as a displayed field.** The list shows the duration it implies — the state left, held that long — and nothing more. The strip remains the instrument for existence boundaries.

**Filtering and paging the list.** The ledger is sparse by design; a hand-curated estate's year of transitions is dozens of rows, not thousands. Machinery for a problem the design does not have.

**URL state for the panel.** The page has no URL surface, and the panel's selection is a session transient, not a shareable address. Revisit: never, short of the router the page refuses.
