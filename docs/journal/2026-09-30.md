Combined design, planning, and implementation session against `uptime-calculation`. Single-agent with terminus gates, two stages: the API, its Go arithmetic, and the page's matching sum first; then the rendering. Stage 1 is built; stage 2 is not started.

## Decisions whose rationale the tree does not show

- **Uptime is `ok` over watched time, and `late` counts against it.** My recommendation, standing as the default. The strip paints `late` as trouble and a figure beside it should agree with the colors; "not failed" would flatter a passive job that is chronically late. Unwatched time and time before a check existed leave the denominator, and the page will say how much was watched.
- **The fact lives on the daemon — Michael's call, reversing my page-side recommendation.** The census puts facts on the daemon and the future MCP reader needs the API to answer "what is the uptime". I had recommended page-side for a mechanical reason that survives the move, below.
- **The API serves durations, not a ratio.** Durations add. A consumer forms whatever ratio it wants, and the page can extend the tail; a ratio can do neither.
- **The walk runs over whole-second instants.** ogen encodes date-times as RFC 3339 without a fractional part, so the daemon truncates to seconds before counting. Otherwise a consumer recomputing from the document could never match the served figures, and the fixture pin below would be impossible. The truncation is pinned by a test case.
- **The count reads the described document, not the engine's view.** `describeUptime` takes `*api.History` and `api.CheckHistory` — the shapes a consumer sees — so it is provably the same walk a consumer can make. It runs after every check is described, because it needs the document's `daemon` events and `watching_at_from`.

## The complication the page will have to answer (stage 2)

A quiet estate refetches never, and the display window slides while the held document does not. A daemon figure over `[from, to]` therefore diverges from the strip beside it: during an outage there are no transitions and no refetch, so the strip shows red growing for days while a served ratio would stay at its onset value; and incidents that have aged out of the strip's window still count for as long as the document is held. The strip copes because bands clip and extend; a scalar cannot. So the page sums its own intervals for display — `uptimeOf` over the same `buildStrip` output it draws — and the daemon's figures are for consumers. That is two statements of one rule, which is why the shared fixture exists.

## Couplings that span files

- **`internal/api/specs/fixtures/history-uptime.json` is read by both suites.** Its uptime figures were worked by hand, not generated by the code under test: a ten-day window, a six-hour daemon outage on the third day, and five checks covering the shapes of the walk (events splitting a state, born mid-window, eventless and quiet, late-only, never existed). The Go test recomputes every check from the document's other fields and requires equality with the figures carried, and `ui/src/uptime.test.ts` does the same through `buildStrip` and `uptimeOf` over the document's own window. The vitest half was pulled into stage 1 on a terminus advisory (`uptime-fixture-not-shared`): the docs called the fixture shared while only the Go half existed, and making it true was cheaper than rewording four places twice. The page reads the fixture through vite's `?raw` import rather than `node:fs`, because the app tsconfig carries no node types and adding them for one test was not worth it. A change to either walk that breaks the other shows up as one of the two suites failing against a file neither of them wrote.
- **`describeUptime` in `internal/server/uptime.go` and `buildStrip` plus `uptimeOf` in `ui/src/history.ts` are one walk in two languages.** Same band rules (state at from, split at events, blank until the first event's `prev_since`, tail from the pair), same gap rules (the opening flag toggled through lifecycle events, a trailing stop running to the end), gap always wins. A rule changed on one side without the other is exactly what the fixture catches.
- **The vitest builders in `detail.test.ts` and `history.test.ts` carry a zero `uptime`** because the contract made the field required. The page's own walk never reads it; the field is there to satisfy the type.
