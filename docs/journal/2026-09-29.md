# 2026-09-29

Combined design, planning, and implementation session against two cards Michael opened together: `layout-compaction` and `separate-detail-pages`. They are one move — pulling the strip into the row removes the anchor the row-expansion panel hung from, and the route is where the panel goes instead. Threshold call: single-agent with a terminus gate, no spec. Built in two stages, the route first. Stage 1 is built and terminus-`clean`; stage 2, the compaction, is not started.

## Decisions whose rationale the tree does not show

- **Path route, not hash route — Michael's call, reversing my first recommendation.** The hash route's only real virtue was zero Go change. The path route won on house precedent (astrometrics is the same shape: an index and one detail at `/groups/{id}`, a hand-rolled pathname matcher, no router library), on the server being able to answer a genuine 404 where a fragment never leaves the browser, and on links surviving mail rewriters once a notifier ever carries one.
- **Shape-only fallback, not registry-validated — Michael's call.** The asset handler serves the index for any well-formed `/check/<id>` and the page reports an unknown id. Validating against the registry would make the asset handler know the configured checks, a coupling bought for one 404.
- **This is the adjudicated route addition the `listener-isolation` quality asks for.** Its boundary says a route added to either surface is a design change, not sanctioned by being small. The adjudication happened in conversation and is recorded in the seam census.
- **`internal/server` restates the check-id pattern rather than importing `internal/config`.** Only `cmd` imports `config` today, and one regex was not a reason to change that. The cost is the three-place coupling below.
- **The default preset still reuses the strip's document, though its old reason is gone.** The law was written as "the same cut as the row beneath it", and there is no longer a row beneath it. The reuse stays because it is one fewer document and the check's page agrees with the estate row the reader just left.

## Couplings that span files

- **The check-id shape is stated three times:** `checkIDPattern` in `internal/config/config.go` (the origin), `checkRoute` in `internal/server/assets.go`, and `checkPath` in `ui/src/route.ts`. Each carries a comment naming the others. The failure if they drift is quiet: a configured check whose page 404s on reload, or routes on the page to a path the daemon refuses. The Go and vitest near-miss tables are the same list on purpose.
- **`navigate()` dispatches `popstate` synchronously, and `App`'s one listener depends on it.** The listener writes `openRef` before it sets route state, so every route change — the page's links and the back button alike — moves what the poll loop reads in one place. Replacing the synchronous dispatch with a state setter, or moving the ref write into an effect, reopens the shape of the 08-19 `presetRef` bug.

## Deliberate non-actions

- **The preset is not in the URL.** Every window is now-anchored, so a preset in a link names a span and never an incident; and a preset arriving by navigation would have to satisfy the landing rule's synchronous-readability requirement on a second path. It stays a click-driven session transient, carried across checks and reset to `90d` by a fresh load.
- **No rollup banner on the check page.** Someone parked on a check's page does not see trouble elsewhere. Left for Michael's placement pass rather than decided here.
- **The v0.1.0 changelog entry still describes the panel as expanding beneath the row.** That is what v0.1.0 shipped; the `Unreleased` entry carries the change.

## Canon drift, flagged and not edited

`dashboard-law` in `terminus-canon` has wording drift only; no clause is invalidated by the route.

- Clauses 11 and 12, and the matching `why` paragraph, say "the row beneath it". The check page has no row beneath it; the reuse rule itself stands.
- Clauses 13 and 14 and the discrimination text speak of the panel being "open" and of "the click that abandoned the window". Open now means the route names a check, and a route change can arrive by the back button as well as a click. Clause 13's synchronous-readability sentence already covers the mechanism; its example of the failure ("a ref or state updated in a later effect than the click") could name the route listener too.

## Live state

- **Stage 2, the compaction, is next and unbuilt.** Proposed and not yet confirmed by Michael: the strip becomes a `history` column between `check` and `in state` at a fixed width near 11rem, row padding trims modestly, and the strip row goes away. `placeIntervals` is container-relative, so no geometry math changes; at that width 90 days is about two pixels a day, so more short incidents will draw as markers.
- **Michael's eye pass on the check page has not happened.** I looked at the estate, a check's page, and the unknown-check page in headless chromium against a scratch daemon; that checks that they render on a direct load, not that they look right.
- **A cheap way to get a strip worth judging:** a scratch daemon pointed at a generated ledger and a matching state file, ending in a `stop` a minute before boot so the daemon appends its own `start`. The ledger must be chronological and the state file's `since` and `state` must agree with the ledger's last event per check. The generator lived in the session scratchpad and was not kept.
